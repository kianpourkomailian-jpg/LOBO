# UK Data Centre Boom — Market Brief for LOBO Systems

_Prepared: 2026-06-16. Opportunity assessment for selling LOBO modular work-at-height
access platforms into UK data centre construction, fit-out, and maintenance._

## The idea, reframed
LOBO sells a modular, no-tools, reconfigurable work-at-height access platform — a faster,
lighter alternative to scaffolding and a more flexible one than MEWPs/towers. The question
assessed: **is the UK data-centre boom a target market worth pursuing, and who do we sell to?**

## Market reality
- UK pipeline ~£36.4bn across ~100 developments; data centres now designated **Critical
  National Infrastructure**; early-stage projects up from ~2GW (2025) to ~8GW (2026).
- Installed IT load forecast to ~double to ~8.4GW by 2031.
- **Where LOBO fits:** data halls are full of repetitive overhead M&E work — cable-tray/
  containment, busbar, overhead power/data cabling, cooling pipework, CRAC/CRAH + ductwork,
  fire suppression, lighting. Industry is shifting to **overhead cable trays**, i.e. more
  sustained work-at-height. Fits both the **build** (M&E install/fit-out) and **operate**
  (lifetime maintenance) phases.
- **Timing hook:** acute M&E/MEP subcontractor labour shortage + rising M&E labour costs.
  LOBO's pitch (assemble faster, no tools, fewer man-hours, less skilled-labour reliance)
  is a painkiller in the year the pain peaks.

## Pros
1. Huge, multi-year, funded demand with CNI protection — visibility to 2030+.
2. Strong product-problem fit in congested data halls (modular access beats scaffold/MEWP).
3. Perfect macro timing — labour shortage makes "fewer man-hours" the #1 buying criterion.
4. Recurring revenue — operators maintain CRAC, overhead cabling, lighting for building life.
5. Land-and-expand — templated hyperscale buildings repeat across a portfolio.
6. Geographic concentration — Slough/West London, M4 corridor, Greater Manchester clusters.
7. Built-in credibility — LOBO already serves NASA, Boeing, nuclear, energy.

## Cons / risks
1. Entrenched incumbents (scaffolding firms, MEWP/IPAF hire) already on every site.
2. Long, relationship-led cycles; must get designed into temporary-works method statements.
3. Contractor concentration — few high-stakes M&E accounts, slow to switch.
4. Capital/operational intensity if rental-at-scale.
5. Timing slippage — grid/power is the real bottleneck; capacity can slip.
6. High-security CNI sites — demos/trials harder to arrange.

## Decision: GO — focused beachhead
- Lead with **M&E sub-contractors** (Tier A) — sharpest labour pain, value prop lands hardest.
- Position as labour-saving + schedule-compressing + work-at-height-safe vs scaffold/MEWP.
- Concentrate on **Slough/West London, M4 corridor (South Wales), Greater Manchester**.
- Parallel "operate" track into operator FM/maintenance teams (Tier C) for recurring rental.
- Prove on 1–2 named live projects → case studies → land-and-expand.
